# Cardiac Output and Venous Return

**Function:** Matches circulatory delivery to tissue demand within heart-vessel constraints.

**Where:** Heart and systemic/pulmonary circulations.

## Mechanism

1. Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume.
2. Venous pressure gradients return blood to the heart; venous tone and blood volume affect filling.
3. Within limits, greater end-diastolic fibre length increases developed force through length-dependent activation.
4. Contractility and afterload alter ejection; output and venous return become equal at steady state.

## Control and constraints

Raising heart rate indefinitely does not indefinitely raise output because filling time and efficiency change. The Frank-Starling mechanism is not the same process as capillary Starling fluid exchange.
