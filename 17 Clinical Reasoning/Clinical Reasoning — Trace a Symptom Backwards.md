# Clinical Reasoning — Trace a Symptom Backwards

Use the physiology map to generate and distinguish mechanisms, without assuming one symptom identifies one cause.

1. Define the observation precisely: concentration, rate, volume, electrical pattern, sensation or behaviour.
2. Separate **production, input, distribution, clearance and measurement effects**. A high concentration can reflect less water as well as more solute.
3. Trace candidate pathways through organ, cell, receptor, signalling and reaction levels.
4. Identify the feedback response expected from each candidate. Distinguish a primary change from compensation.
5. Ask what observation would discriminate between plausible mechanisms and what time scale it should follow.
6. Check physiological state, medications/exposures, measurement timing and relevant clinical context.

| Observation | Mechanistically distinct possibilities |
|---|---|
| Higher heart rate | Autonomic drive, intrinsic pacemaker effects, compensation for reduced stroke volume, endocrine influences and others |
| Lower plasma sodium | Altered water balance relative to exchangeable solute; not automatically depleted total sodium |
| Reduced oxygen delivery | Low haemoglobin, low saturation, low cardiac output or impaired distribution |
| More lactate | Increased production, altered oxidation/clearance or mixed mechanisms; not automatically absent oxygen |

The vault does not provide diagnostic thresholds, treatment instructions or a substitute for assessment. Start with [[Homeostatic Variables]], [[Clinical Measurements — MOC]] and [[Evidence and Scope]].
