# Carbon Dioxide Transport

**Function:** Carries metabolic carbon dioxide to lungs while participating in acid-base buffering.

**Where:** Tissues, plasma and erythrocytes.

## Mechanism

1. CO2 diffuses from tissues into blood and erythrocytes.
2. Carbonic anhydrase rapidly interconverts CO2 and bicarbonate; haemoglobin buffers much of the generated H+.
3. AE1 exchanges bicarbonate and chloride across the red-cell membrane.
4. In lungs the reactions favour CO2 release, aided by oxygenation-related changes in haemoglobin buffering.

## Control and constraints

Most transported CO2 is bicarbonate; smaller fractions are dissolved or carbamino-bound. The Haldane effect concerns oxygenation-dependent CO2 carriage; the Bohr effect concerns H+/CO2 effects on oxygen affinity.
