# Insulin and Glucagon

**Function:** Coordinates storage and mobilisation of fuels across fed and fasting states.

**Where:** Pancreatic islets, liver, muscle and adipose tissue.

## Mechanism

1. Beta-cell glucose metabolism raises ATP/ADP, closes KATP channels and drives Ca2+-dependent insulin secretion.
2. Insulin receptor signalling promotes tissue-specific glucose uptake and storage while restraining hepatic glucose production and adipose lipolysis.
3. Low glucose and amino-acid/autonomic signals influence alpha-cell glucagon release.
4. Glucagon acts predominantly on liver through cAMP pathways to support glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.

## Control and constraints

Human beta cells commonly rely strongly on GLUT1/GLUT3 rather than the rodent GLUT2-dominant model. Skeletal muscle does not use glucagon as a major direct glycogenolytic signal. Islet paracrine interactions shape both hormones.
