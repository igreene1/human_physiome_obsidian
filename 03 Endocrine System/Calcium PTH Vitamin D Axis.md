# Calcium PTH Vitamin D Axis

**Function:** Coordinates ionised calcium, phosphate and mineral supply.

**Where:** Parathyroids, kidneys, bone and intestine.

## Mechanism

1. Low extracellular ionised calcium reduces calcium-sensing-receptor activity and promotes PTH secretion.
2. PTH increases renal calcium retention while decreasing proximal phosphate reabsorption.
3. PTH supports renal calcitriol formation; calcitriol increases intestinal calcium/phosphate absorption.
4. Bone-cell signalling adjusts turnover, and restored calcium suppresses PTH.

## Control and constraints

FGF23 from bone promotes phosphate excretion and reduces calcitriol production. PTH acts on osteoblast-lineage cells to regulate osteoclasts, rather than directly activating osteoclast PTH receptors as the main mechanism.
