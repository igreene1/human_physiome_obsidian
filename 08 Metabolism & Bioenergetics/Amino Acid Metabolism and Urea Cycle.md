# Amino Acid Metabolism and Urea Cycle

**Function:** Redistributes amino nitrogen, uses carbon skeletons and disposes of excess nitrogen.

**Where:** Multiple tissues; hepatic urea cycle spans mitochondria and cytosol.

## Mechanism

1. PLP-dependent transaminases exchange nitrogen with keto acids, often involving glutamate.
2. Glutamate/glutamine pathways transport or release nitrogen according to tissue needs.
3. CPS1 and OTC form citrulline in mitochondria; cytosolic ASS1, ASL and ARG1 produce urea.
4. Kidneys excrete urea, while carbon skeletons enter oxidative or biosynthetic pathways.

## Control and constraints

The two urea nitrogens derive from free ammonia and aspartate. Glutamine supports safe nitrogen transport, biosynthesis and renal ammonium excretion. Amino acids differ in whether their carbon is glucogenic, ketogenic or both.
