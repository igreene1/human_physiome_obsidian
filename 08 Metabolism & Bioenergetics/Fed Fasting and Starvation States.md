# Fed Fasting and Starvation States

**Function:** Redistributes substrate use across changing food availability.

**Where:** Liver, muscle, adipose tissue, brain, red cells and kidneys.

## Mechanism

1. After feeding, nutrient absorption and insulin favour glycogen, lipid and protein synthesis.
2. Early fasting uses hepatic glycogen and increasing gluconeogenesis to support circulating glucose.
3. Lower insulin permits adipose lipolysis; fatty-acid oxidation supports hepatic energy needs and ketogenesis.
4. With prolonged fasting, brain ketone use rises and substrate partitioning helps conserve protein.

## Control and constraints

Transitions are gradual and depend on prior diet, activity and stores. Red cells continue to require glycolysis. Muscle glycogen is a local store and is not directly released as circulating free glucose.
