# ATP Generation

**Function:** Couples energy-releasing processes to synthesis of ATP for cellular work.

**Where:** Cytosol and mitochondria; proportions depend on cell type.

## Mechanism

1. Substrate-level phosphorylation transfers phosphate directly to ADP or GDP in selected metabolic reactions.
2. Oxidative pathways generate reducing equivalents that feed mitochondrial electron transport.
3. The respiratory chain generates a proton-motive force, driving ATP synthase.
4. Creatine kinase and adenylate kinase buffer rapid changes in nucleotide demand.

## Control and constraints

ATP is continuously recycled, not a long-term energy store. Mature erythrocytes lack mitochondria. Complete glucose oxidation is often estimated near 30-32 ATP, but actual yield depends on shuttles, proton leak and cellular costs.
